# First Midas Wedge Decision

## Decision

The first Midas Expansion Radar wedge will be:

> **U.S. food-and-beverage manufacturing expansion signals routed to qualified industrial refrigeration, HVAC, and process-utility providers.**

This is a working pilot decision, not a permanent market commitment. The purpose is to test whether a public or otherwise permitted expansion signal can be turned into a useful, high-value provider conversation.

## Geography

The initial operating geography will be a compact U.S. Southeast cluster:

- Georgia.
- Tennessee.
- North Carolina.

The cluster is intentionally large enough to produce a meaningful account universe but narrow enough for manual evidence review, provider mapping, and operational learning. We can expand to South Carolina, Virginia, Texas, the Midwest, or a national view only after the first workflow has stable quality and clear economics.

The United States is the initial market because it offers a larger potential signal universe, more provider density, and a practical English-language operating environment. Sweden and the broader Nordics remain attractive future modules, especially where local knowledge, language, and relationships can create an advantage.

## Industry

The initial industry is **food-and-beverage manufacturing**, including selected subsectors such as beverages, prepared foods, dairy, cold-chain production, meat processing, and related packaging operations.

The industry is a useful test category because it combines physical facilities, equipment, maintenance, production continuity, logistics, staffing, and compliance needs. A BLS Monthly Labor Review article published in June 2026 describes food-and-beverage manufacturing as a large and growing U.S. manufacturing category and projects it to add the most jobs of any manufacturing sector over 2024–34 [1]. That context supports the category, but it is not itself an opportunity signal; Midas still requires company-specific evidence.

## Provider category

The first provider category is:

> **Industrial refrigeration, HVAC, and process-utility contractors capable of serving food-and-beverage manufacturing facilities.**

The provider profile should favor companies that can handle one or more of the following:

- Industrial refrigeration or cold-chain systems.
- Process cooling, heating, ventilation, or air handling.
- Plant utility installation, retrofit, or maintenance.
- Preventive maintenance and emergency response.
- Commissioning or expansion support for production facilities.

We are not initially selling “all contractors.” We are looking for providers with a defined service area, credible proof, adequate capacity, and a transaction size large enough to justify a focused pipeline effort.

## Why this provider category

The provider category is intentionally close to the expansion event. When a food or beverage manufacturer announces a new site, capacity addition, production line, or major facility investment, industrial refrigeration, HVAC, and process utilities are plausible operational requirements. The signal does not prove a purchase, but it creates a defensible reason to investigate a need.

The category also gives Midas a concrete provider-side value proposition:

> **We identify food-and-beverage facilities in your target states showing credible expansion or investment signals, verify the operating context, and route the most relevant opportunities to your team.**

## First signal types

The first version will track only a few event types:

| Signal type | Evidence examples | Initial interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| New or expanded facility | Company announcement, filing, official project page, public agency announcement | Potential need for new industrial systems, installation, commissioning, and maintenance. |
| Production-capacity investment | Filing, press release, official announcement, capital-project notice | Potential need for equipment, utilities, retrofit, and maintenance. |
| Acquisition or geographic entry | Filing or company announcement | Potential need to standardize or upgrade facilities and suppliers. |
| New production line or major modernization | Official company statement, project announcement, public permit or procurement evidence | Potential need for process utilities, controls, refrigeration, and specialist contractors. |
| Supporting hiring evidence | Company career page or permitted job-posting source | Corroborating evidence that expansion is operationally active. |

A signal will not be ranked highly solely because it contains words such as “growth” or “investment.” It must identify a company, relevant geography, evidence, date, and a plausible facility-level transaction hypothesis.

## First transaction hypothesis

The initial transaction is a **qualified provider conversation**, not an automatic sale and not a claim that the buyer is ready to purchase.

The working flow is:

```text
Expansion evidence
→ facility and company verification
→ likely facility need hypothesis
→ provider-fit check
→ human review
→ relevant introduction or outreach
→ conversation with provider or buyer-side contact
→ outcome and value capture
```

In the first pilot, Midas may route in either direction depending on access and consent:

1. **Provider acquisition route:** identify a provider that wants expansion-related opportunities, then research and approach the relevant manufacturing account.
2. **Opportunity route:** identify the manufacturing expansion first, then determine which qualified providers can help and seek an appropriate introduction.

The second route is closer to the long-term Midas thesis. The first route may be faster for acquiring an initial paying provider client. The system should support both without confusing the buyer-side account with the provider-side account.

## Initial account and provider constraints

### Manufacturing account

The first account universe should be limited to organizations with:

- A facility or operating presence in Georgia, Tennessee, or North Carolina.
- A food, beverage, prepared-food, dairy, meat, cold-chain, or related manufacturing profile.
- A recent, evidence-backed expansion, modernization, new-site, or capacity signal.
- A plausible facility, production, or utility need.

### Provider

The first provider universe should be limited to companies with:

- A service area covering at least part of the selected geography.
- Industrial, not merely residential, capability.
- Food, beverage, cold-chain, manufacturing, or process-utility experience where possible.
- A credible minimum project size and response capacity.
- A named owner for opportunities and follow-up.

## What would make us change the wedge

We should change the wedge if, after a controlled sample, one of these conditions appears:

- Expansion events are too rare, too stale, or too difficult to verify.
- Providers cannot explain why the signals create valuable opportunities.
- The provider category is too crowded, too slow, or unable to accept new work.
- The buyer-side contact cannot be identified or reached through an appropriate channel.
- The transaction value does not support the research and routing cost.
- Another signal family produces materially better evidence, speed, or conversion.

## Next action

Build a small sample of expansion records for the selected states and subsectors. Each record should include the source, event date, evidence summary, account, facility or geography, event type, provider need hypothesis, and initial score. Before any scaled outreach, have a second reviewer assess signal accuracy and provider relevance.
